# Best TinyStories model under 50M parameters

A study of how to spend a **total** parameter budget, which is a different question from every A/B
in [docs/results.md](../../docs/results.md). Those match *active* parameters, or let total params
float entirely: the loop-vs-depth arms range from 31.8M to 79.1M, and the winner, arm C, is the
biggest one on the page. Under a hard total cap, "make it bigger" stops being available and the
question becomes which 50M parameters to buy.

Two things change under the cap, and both invert a conclusion the repo currently holds:

1. **The embedding becomes a bidding opponent, not a background cost.** The tied
   token-embedding matrix is `vocab_size * d_model`, and with gpt2's 50k vocab that is 12.9M
   parameters at `d_model: 256`, 19.3M at 384, 25.8M at 512 — 27-52% of the budget, spent before
   a single block exists. Widening the model therefore costs twice. `radiance-size` (added for
   this study) prints this split.
2. **The loop is nearly free in parameters.** `blocks[1:]` is weight-shared, so `loop_count: 4`
   buys 3.5x the executed blocks for a few thousand parameters of per-iteration conditioning
   (pinned in `tests/test_size.py`). docs/results.md concludes the loop is *dominated* — but that
   verdict is cost-counted, at equal FLOPs, where depth is the rival. Under a parameter cap with
   compute unconstrained the loop's rival is different, and the verdict may not survive.

## Method

Standard hygiene from [docs/results.md](../../docs/results.md)'s cautions, applied throughout:
`batch_size` pinned at 16 x `grad_accum_steps` 2 with `auto_batch_size: false` (caution 1),
`dropout: 0.0`, wandb disabled, one arm at a time on the whole GPU (caution 3), every arm sharing
`max_steps` so no two runs are read against each other mid-schedule (caution 6), and a per-arm LR
sweep before any architecture claim (caution 3 again — the arms' LR curves cross).

Deviation from the prior experiments, stated so the numbers are not mixed with theirs: these runs
are `dtype: bf16`, where loop-vs-depth ran the `fp32` default. This study is self-contained and
carries its own baselines, but **its `val/loss` values are not comparable to docs/results.md's**.

Configs are generated by `gen.py` and run serially by `run.sh` (both carried over from
[../loop-vs-depth/](../loop-vs-depth/)).

### Shape arms

Nine dense shapes spanning the width/depth trade, every one sized with `radiance-size` to land in
46.5-49.1M so no arm can win by being bigger:

| arm | `d_model` | `n_layers` | `ffn_mult` | total | embed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `d256_L24_fm3` | 256 | 24 | 3.0 | 47.61M | 12.87M |
| `d256_L16_fm4` | 256 | 16 | 4.0 | 46.53M | 12.87M |
| `d320_L14_fm3` | 320 | 14 | 3.0 | 47.73M | 16.10M |
| `d320_L10_fm4` | 320 | 10 | 4.0 | 48.94M | 16.10M |
| `d384_L9_fm3` | 384 | 9 | 3.0 | 48.59M | 19.32M |
| `d384_L6_fm4` | 384 | 6 | 4.0 | 47.69M | 19.32M |
| `d448_L6_fm3` | 448 | 6 | 3.0 | 49.09M | 22.53M |
| `d448_L4_fm4` | 448 | 4 | 4.0 | 48.27M | 22.53M |
| `d512_L4_fm3` | 512 | 4 | 3.0 | 48.87M | 25.76M |

### The vocabulary axis, and why it needs a different metric

Shrinking the tokenizer is the largest single source of free budget: a tokenizer trained on
TinyStories itself (`train_tokenizer.py`) frees 10.9M parameters at `d_model: 320` against gpt2's
50k, and 21.6M at `d_model: 512` — up to 43% of the whole budget, redeployable as depth or width.
Four sizes were tried (4k, 8k, 16k, 32k); **16k wins**, and the sections below build up to that.

**`val/loss` cannot measure this.** Cross-entropy is per token, and a smaller vocabulary cuts the
same story into more, individually easier tokens, so it lowers per-token loss for reasons that have
nothing to do with model quality; taken at face value it ranks a character-level model above every
BPE model. `bits_per_char.py` scores total NLL over a fixed span of text divided by that text's
character count, which is invariant to segmentation. Any number in this directory produced with a
non-gpt2 tokenizer is reported in bits/char, and bits/char is never compared against a `val/loss`.

## Results

### The tokenizer is free budget (measured, no training required)

The first result needed no training run at all. Three byte-level BPE tokenizers were trained on
TinyStories (`train_tokenizer.py`) and their compression measured against gpt2's on 3000 held-out
validation stories:

| tokenizer | vocab | chars/token | embed @ `d_model: 384` | embed @ 512 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gpt2 | 50,257 | 4.08 | 19.32M | 25.76M |
| `ts16k` | 16,384 | **4.19** | 6.29M | 8.39M |
| `ts8k` | 8,192 | **4.15** | 3.15M | 4.19M |
| `ts4k` | 4,096 | 3.98 | 1.57M | 2.10M |

**A domain-trained 8k or 16k vocabulary is more compressive than gpt2's 50k on this corpus, not
less.** gpt2 spends most of its vocabulary on code, other languages and rare words that never occur
in three-year-old-level English, so the great majority of its rows are dead weight that the tied
embedding pays for in full. Dropping 50k -> 16k frees **13.0M parameters at `d_model: 384`** while
*shortening* sequences slightly, so it does not even cost the extra compute a smaller vocabulary
normally implies. Only at 4k does compression finally get worse than gpt2's, which is the first
hint that the optimum is interior rather than "as small as possible".

What that buys, sized with `radiance-size`: under gpt2 the cap allows `d384` at `n_layers: 6`;
under `ts8k` the same width and budget allows `n_layers: 14`.

The quality half of this still has to be measured in bits/char (see above) — this table establishes
the budget and the sequence-length cost, not that the resulting model is better.

### LR sweep: the optimum moves with shape, and the grid was too small

1500 steps, three shapes spanning the width/depth span. `val/loss` at step 1500:

| arm | `lr` 3e-3 | 1e-2 | 3e-2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `d256_L24_fm3` (deep, narrow) | 1.6787 | **1.6706** | 1.6827 |
| `d384_L6_fm4` (middle) | 1.6942 | 1.6956 | **1.6835** |
| `d512_L4_fm3` (shallow, wide) | 1.6966 | **1.6865** | 1.6960 |

The optimum is at `1e-2` for the deep arm and at `3e-2` for the middle one, and the within-arm
spread (up to 0.012) is the same size as the between-arm shape effect being measured (0.02). So a
single shared LR would have been able to support either verdict — caution 3 reproducing exactly,
on this study's own arms rather than borrowed from the record.

`d384_L6`'s optimum also landed on the **top edge of the grid**, which measures the grid rather
than the optimum. The sweep was extended to `1e-1` and to all nine shapes rather than three;
under-tuning the wide arms specifically is how a width/depth sweep talks itself into "depth wins".

Extended, `1e-1` is decisively bad for every arm, so the grid brackets each optimum on both sides.
The structure that falls out is that **the optimum tracks `ffn_mult`, not depth**: every `fm3` arm
wants `1e-2` and every `fm4` arm wants `3e-2`, while depth varies 4 -> 24 within those groups
without moving it. The per-shape values are recorded in `gen.py`'s `BEST_LR` so the later stages
cannot silently be run at one shared LR.

### Shape at 8000 steps: the 1500-step ranking inverts

Nine shapes, each at its own swept LR, plus a seed-43 repeat of the leader for a noise floor:

| arm | @1500 | @8000 |
|---|---|---|
| **`d256_L24_fm3`** | 1.6706 (3rd) | **1.4191** |
| **`d320_L14_fm3`** | 1.6691 (2nd) | **1.4192** |
| `d448_L6_fm3` | 1.6684 (**1st**) | 1.4363 |
| `d448_L6_fm3` (seed 43) | — | 1.4340 |
| `d320_L10_fm4` | 1.6720 | 1.4395 |
| `d384_L9_fm3` | 1.6797 | 1.4433 |
| `d256_L16_fm4` | 1.6834 | 1.4499 |
| `d512_L4_fm3` | 1.6865 | 1.4502 |
| `d384_L6_fm4` | 1.6835 | 1.4539 |
| `d448_L4_fm4` | 1.6889 | 1.4640 |

The seed repeat (1.4363 vs 1.4340) suggested a noise floor of 0.0023 at this point. **That figure
is superseded** — see "The noise floor is 0.0021, and it is not seed variation" below, where five
identical runs put the stdev at 0.0021 and the *range* at 0.0042. Rankings in this table separated
by less than ~0.006 should not be read as rankings.

The arm that led at 1500 steps finished **7th at 8000**, 0.017 behind — 7x the noise floor. This is
caution 1 reproducing on this study's own arms, and it is the reason the nine-arm sweep was run in
full rather than pruned to the 1500-step top five as originally planned.

### Extending the shape grid, because the winner sat on its edge

`d256_L24_fm3` was the deepest shape in the grid, so the grid had measured its own boundary — the
same failure the LR stage hit at `3e-2`, one stage later. Six deeper shapes were added, buying
depth by narrowing the FFN rather than the residual stream: `ffn_mult` trades against `n_layers`
far more cheaply than `d_model` does, because `d_model` also drags the embedding, which is already
a third of the budget.

All six prefer `lr: 1e-2` (interior), and at 1500 steps all but one already beat every round-1
shape — the best round-1 arm scored 1.6684, against:

| arm | `3e-3` | `1e-2` | `3e-2` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `d256_L48_fm1.5` | 1.6756 | **1.6577** | 1.6663 |
| `d320_L22_fm2` | 1.6694 | **1.6590** | 1.6728 |
| `d256_L37_fm2` | 1.6728 | **1.6633** | 1.6702 |
| `d320_L17_fm2.5` | 1.6728 | **1.6671** | 1.6720 |
| `d256_L29_fm2.5` | 1.6772 | **1.6725** | 1.6768 |
| `d192_L46_fm3` | 1.6943 | **1.6851** | 1.6910 |

`d192_L46_fm3` is 0.027 behind the best of these, >10x noise, which brackets width from *below*:
`d_model: 192` is too narrow whatever depth it buys. Combined with round 1's wide arms losing from
above, the optimum is enclosed at `d256`-`d320` rather than sitting on a boundary. It is dropped
from the 8000-step round on that basis.

At 8000 steps the deeper shapes land essentially on top of round 1's winners:

| arm | @8000 |
|---|---|
| `d320_L22_fm2` | 1.4163 |
| `d256_L37_fm2` | 1.4166 |
| `d256_L48_fm1.5` | 1.4172 |
| `d256_L24_fm3` (round 1) | 1.4191 |
| `d320_L14_fm3` (round 1) | 1.4192 |
| `d320_L17_fm2.5` | 1.4198 |
| `d256_L29_fm2.5` | 1.4211 |

**Shape is a plateau, not a peak.** At `d256`, going L16 -> L24 is worth 0.031; going L24 -> L48 is
worth nothing (±0.003). Everything from L14 to L48 at `d256`-`d320` sits inside the noise floor
established below, so the shape decision is close to free once you are in the deep-narrow region —
and the remaining gains have to come from somewhere other than shape.

### The noise floor is 0.0021, and it is not seed variation

The seed-43 repeat above suggested 0.0023 from n=2. Then the feature stage's baseline — the *same
config, same seed 42* as `d320_L22_fm2` in the shape sweep, differing only in `run_name` and
`output_dir` — scored 1.4122 against 1.4163. Five identical runs:

| run | @8000 |
|---|---|
| shape sweep | 1.4163 |
| feature stage `base` | 1.4122 |
| `base_r1` | 1.4124 |
| `base_r2` | 1.4157 |
| `base_r3` | 1.4121 |

**mean 1.4137, stdev 0.0021, range 0.0042** — with the seed pinned throughout, so this is GPU
nondeterminism (non-deterministic reductions under `torch.compile`/`flex_attention`), not seed
choice. The relevant figure for ranking two single runs is the stdev of their *difference*,
`sqrt(2) * 0.0021 = 0.0030`, so a gap needs to clear roughly 0.006 to be worth reporting.

The practical lesson is that **a repeat at a different seed understates this**: it measures seed
variation plus nondeterminism from n=2, and n=2 on a 0.002 stdev is close to uninformative. Every
comparison below is made against the 5-run mean rather than against any single baseline run.

### Features at 8000 steps, against the 5-run base mean of 1.4137

| arm | @8000 | vs base | total / active | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `loop2_deep` | **1.4044** | −0.0093 | 47.88M | real (~3σ) |
| `diff_attn` | 1.4086 | −0.0051 | 47.77M | ~1.7σ, **not established** |
| `loop_w512` | 1.4127 | −0.0010 | 48.12M | neutral |
| `loop_w448` | 1.4154 | +0.0017 | 45.30M | neutral |
| `mqa_L24` | 1.4273 | +0.0136 | 46.70M | real regression |
| `mtp2_L21` | 1.4313 | +0.0176 | 47.97M | real regression |
| `moe_d256_L14` | 1.4356 | +0.0219 | 45.82M / 25.30M | real regression |
| `moe_d320_L8` | 1.4424 | +0.0287 | 45.30M / 28.05M | real regression |

**MoE inverts under a total cap.** docs/results.md records `use_moe` as the largest architecture
effect on the page, +0.0488 at matched *active* parameters — and here it is the largest *negative*
effect, −0.022 to −0.029. Both are correct. The earlier A/B holds active parameters fixed and lets
total parameters grow 1.7x, which is exactly the freedom a total cap removes: forced to buy its
experts out of depth and width, MoE lands at 25-28M active against the dense arms' 47.8M, and the
capacity it gains does not repay the compute it gives up. The result is constraint-dependent, and
the constraint the repo measured under is not the one a "model under 50M parameters" imposes.

**The loop splits by which question is asked**, and both answers are visible in one table:

- At **equal compute** (`loop_w448`/`loop_w512`: ~22 executed blocks, weight-sharing spent on
  reaching `d448`/`d512` where the dense base can only afford `d320`), the loop is *neutral* —
  −0.0010 and +0.0017, both inside noise. Consistent with docs/results.md's "dominated": sharing
  does not buy enough extra width to pay for itself.
- At **equal parameters and free compute** (`loop2_deep`: 43 executed blocks for +0.11M
  parameters), the loop is the best arm measured, −0.0093.

That is not a contradiction of the loop-vs-depth result, it is its complement. A FLOP budget prices
the loop out; a *parameter* budget does not charge for the thing the loop spends. Under this
study's constraint the loop pays.

### The vocabulary result, and the metric inversion that hides it

Each arm spends what the smaller tokenizer frees on depth or width, so all of them still fill the
cap. Scored on the same 4000 held-out stories:

| arm | total | `val/loss` | **bits/char** | vs gpt2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gpt2 `d320_L22` | 47.77M | 1.4137 | 0.5043 | — |
| `ts8k_d320_L32` | 48.69M | 1.4162 | 0.5006 | −0.0037 |
| `ts8k_d384_L22` | 48.73M | 1.4135 | 0.4997 | −0.0046 |
| `ts32k_d320_L27` | 49.35M | 1.4224 | 0.4993 | −0.0050 |
| **`ts16k_d320_L30`** | 48.43M | 1.4143 | **0.4967** | **−0.0076** |

**`val/loss` cannot see this result.** gpt2's 1.4137 edges `ts16k`'s 1.4143, so judged the way every
other table in this repo is judged the vocabulary change looks worthless — while per character it
is the largest single effect in the study. A smaller vocabulary cuts each story into more,
individually more predictable tokens, so per-token loss falls for reasons unrelated to the model;
only dividing by a unit the tokenizer cannot change compares the arms on the same question.

In these units the noise floor is `0.0021 * 0.246 / ln 2 = 0.00075` bits/char, so `ts16k`'s −0.0076
is about **10x noise**.

**The optimum is interior: 16k beats both 8k and 32k, and all three beat gpt2's 50k.** A vocabulary
can be shrunk past the point where the freed parameters repay the lost compression, so "as small as
possible" is the wrong instinct. `d320_L32` vs `d384_L22` is 0.0009 apart — inside noise — so this
study does *not* separate depth from width once a cheap embedding has made width affordable again.

**What the arms are matched on, stated explicitly.** All process the same number of *tokens* (8000
steps x 16384), i.e. the same compute — not the same number of characters. Because `ts16k`
compresses better (0.2400 tokens/char against gpt2's 0.2460), that same token budget covers ~2.5%
more text. That is deliberate: at equal compute, better compression buying more text is part of
what a tokenizer *is worth*. But it means the −0.0076 is the combined effect of three things the
tokenizer choice causes at once — 10.9M parameters moved from the embedding into eight extra
layers, ~2.5% more text seen, and the segmentation itself — and this study does not decompose them.

### Two measurement bugs found while producing that table

Both were caught by numbers that disagreed with each other rather than by numbers that looked
wrong, which is the argument for computing the same quantity two ways.

**1. The scorer evaluated in a different attention regime than training.** `bits_per_char.py`
originally loaded checkpoints through `generate.load_checkpoint`, which passes `eos_id=None` and
therefore switches document masking *off* — correct for generation, where the prompt is one
document, and wrong here. These models trained with `doc_attention_mask: true`, so scoring them
without it lets every token attend across story boundaries it was never trained to see.

The mismatch is **not uniform across arms**, which is what made it visible: within a single
tokenizer, where `bits/char` must be a monotonic transform of `val/loss`, it produced the *opposite*
ranking of the four `ts16k` arms. The looped arm was penalised hardest. Passing the real
`eos_id` fixed the ordering (it now matches `val/loss` exactly) and moved `nll/token` from 1.6861 to
1.4346, close to the trainer's own figure. Every bits/char number above is post-fix; the pre-fix
numbers reversed the conclusion about whether the features stack.

**2. The validation split is heterogeneous enough to swamp the effects being measured.**
Consecutive 1000-story chunks of `validation` score 1.63, 1.75, 1.58 and 1.79 nats/token under the
same model — a 0.2 spread against effects of 0.01. Scoring 1000 stories instead of 4000 moved
`bits/char` by 3.5%, which initially looked like a bug in the denominator and is in fact the data.
Comparisons stay valid because every arm is scored on the identical story range, but **no absolute
bits/char figure here is portable to a different evaluation slice.**

### Final: the iso-compute controls, which changed the answer

Everything above is fixed-step. The cap in this study is on parameters, so fixed-step is the
*literal* reading of the question — but two of the three winning ingredients buy their gain with
compute the cap does not charge for, and a result that only holds when compute is free should say
so. One epoch (25000 steps) first:

| config | `val/loss` | bits/char |
|---|---|---|
| gpt2 `d320_L22` dense | 1.3151 | 0.4696 |
| `ts16k` + `use_diff_attn` + `loop_count: 2` | **1.3033** | **0.4574** |

−0.0122, ~16x noise, and the margin grew from −0.0104 at 8000 steps, so the three effects stack.
Then the same comparison with wall clock held fixed instead of steps:

| config | steps | wall clock | bits/char |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ts16k` + `use_diff_attn` + `loop_count: 2` | 25000 | 3940s | 0.4574 |
| gpt2 `d320_L22` dense | 59000 | 3908s | 0.4545 |
| `ts16k_d320_L30` dense | 40000 | *2904s* | 0.4535 |
| **`ts16k_d320_L30` dense (shipped)** | 54000 | 3916s | **0.4491** |

**The combination loses at equal compute.** `loop_count: 2` roughly doubles executed blocks and
differential attention adds ~1.34x; given that back, the plain dense control runs 59000 steps
instead of 25000 and finishes ahead. This is the check docs/results.md's differential-attention
section names as unrun — "a 1.3-1.4x step-time cost can plausibly erase a fixed-step win at equal
wall-clock" — and the answer is that it does.

**The tokenizer wins in both currencies**, which is what separates it from the other two. `ts16k`
dense beats the gpt2 dense control on **26% less compute**, because a better-compressing vocabulary
is free by construction: it moves 10.9M parameters out of the embedding *and* shortens sequences.
Of the three ingredients it is the only one that should be carried into a compute-bound setting
without re-measuring, and it is also the largest.

So the shipped `configs/tinystories_50m.yaml` is the tokenizer plus a plain dense stack, with the
loop and differential attention documented as deliberately omitted and the condition under which
they would be added (parameters binding, compute free — an inference-memory or download-size
budget, not a training budget) written down.

### What would be worth doing next

- **Decompose the tokenizer win.** It bundles three things — parameters moved out of the embedding,
  ~2.5% more text seen at equal tokens, and the segmentation itself. A `ts16k` arm held at gpt2's
  `n_layers: 22` would separate the first from the other two.
- **Re-run the vocabulary sweep at `fineweb` scale.** The 16k optimum is a property of TinyStories'
  vocabulary, which is tiny and repetitive; nothing here suggests where the optimum sits on a
  general corpus, and the whole effect could plausibly vanish.
- **Check `loop_count: 2` at a longer horizon.** It was the largest fixed-step architecture effect
  measured and it lost only on step time. If it holds its advantage per *step* as steps grow, there
  is a crossover point; this study did not look for one.
